The Problem

One trade-off drives every pricing decision.

E[Margin/day] =
Ŝt × CR(p) × (p − c)
Ŝ = forecasted daily sessions
CR = conversion rate at price p
c = cost per unit

Lowering price raises CR but compresses margin per unit.
Raising price does the reverse.

The optimal price is the interior solution where the product of CR and unit margin is maximised — and it is different for every destination cluster.

A uniform global price rule overcharges elastic markets (losing conversion) and undercharges inelastic ones (leaving margin on the table).

05 / 08
Hito 3 · Demand Forecasting

Volume scales the value of every pricing decision.

A global GBM model trained on all 50 destinations simultaneously. One model, shared seasonality structure, better generalisation on low-volume destinations.

The same 5% margin improvement at 500 daily sessions (July peak) is worth 2.5× more in absolute terms than at 200 sessions (January trough). Ignoring forecast volume misallocates pricing effort.

Lag features dominate importance — 7-day and 28-day lags are the strongest predictors of near-future demand.

Hito 4 · A/B Testing Framework

The peeking problem is real. And quantified.

1,000 simulations under null hypothesis (no real effect). Stopping at first p<0.05 inflates false positives 4×.

Experiment design for europe_leisure cluster

At cluster level (1,680 sessions/day), detecting a 10% CR lift takes 66 days. At individual destination level (120/day) it takes 924 days — not feasible.
